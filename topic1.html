Introduction to Cyber Threats

A cyber threat is a potential danger to computers, networks, applications, or digital information. Cybercriminals may use deceptive messages, malicious software, or stolen credentials to compromise systems.

Learning to recognize common threats helps individuals and organizations make safer decisions online.

1. Phishing

Phishing is a form of social engineering in which an attacker pretends to be a trusted person or organization. The goal is often to trick someone into revealing passwords, financial information, or other sensitive data.

  • Unexpected messages asking for personal information.
  • Links leading to unfamiliar or misleading websites.
  • Messages creating urgency or threatening consequences.

2. Malware

Malware is software created to harm a system, disrupt its operation, spy on users, or gain unauthorized access. Viruses, spyware, and ransomware are examples of malware.

  • Download software only from trusted sources.
  • Keep your operating system and applications updated.
  • Use reputable security software.

3. Password Attacks

Attackers may guess weak passwords, reuse credentials exposed in data breaches, or use automated techniques to attempt account access.

Unique passwords and multifactor authentication can reduce the risk of unauthorized access.

Comparing Common Threats

Threat Common Method Possible Impact
Phishing Deceptive messages Stolen information
Malware Malicious software Damaged or compromised systems
Password attacks Guessing or stolen credentials Unauthorized account access

Summary

Cyber threats take many forms, but awareness is an important first step toward reducing risk. Recognizing suspicious messages, updating software, and protecting login credentials can improve online safety.
